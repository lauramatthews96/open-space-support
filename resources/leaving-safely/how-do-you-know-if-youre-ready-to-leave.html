How Do You Know If You're Ready to Leave?

6 min read · Last updated August 2026

Maybe you're not planning anything yet. Maybe you don't even know if "leaving" is the right word for what you're feeling, it might just be a quiet, uncomfortable sense that something isn't fitting the way it used to. If that's where you are, this one's for you, not the practical logistics.

There's no test you can take, no checklist that tells you definitively "yes, now." But there are some honest questions worth sitting with, and some things worth knowing about how this stage tends to feel, so you don't mistake a completely normal part of the process for a sign that something's wrong with you.

It's normal to feel ready and not ready at the same time

This is probably the single most common thing people describe: wanting to leave and being terrified to, in the same breath. Feeling certain on a Tuesday and full of doubt by Thursday. That's not confusion or weakness. It's what it actually feels like to consider walking away from something that has shaped your entire life, your relationships, and possibly your understanding of what happens after you die.

You're allowed to feel two contradictory things at once. Most people in your position do.

You don't need to have all the answers before you start questioning

A lot of people put off even letting themselves think seriously about leaving because they feel like they need a fully formed alternative first: a new belief system, a clear plan, certainty about what's true. You don't. Questioning doesn't require you to already know the answer. It's completely possible to sit in genuine uncertainty for a long time, and that's not a failure of the process. It might just be the process.

Some things worth asking yourself, gently

These aren't a test. There's no score. They're just questions that a lot of people find clarifying, in whatever order makes sense to you.

What would I actually lose, and what would I actually gain? Not the version of this question designed to scare you, and not the version designed to talk yourself into anything, just an honest, private accounting. Some losses are real and painful. Some gains are real too, even if they're harder to picture right now.

If I'd encountered all of this for the first time today, as an adult, with no history in it, would I choose it? This isn't about erasing your history or pretending the good parts weren't real. It's just a way of separating "this is familiar" from "this is genuinely what I'd choose."

Am I afraid of leaving, or afraid of the disapproval of leaving? These can feel identical from the inside but they're not the same fear, and it's worth trying to tell them apart.

Do I need to decide anything today? Almost always, the honest answer is no. You're allowed to keep thinking. You're allowed to change your mind in either direction as many times as you need to.

The sunk cost feeling is real, and it's not a character flaw

If part of what's keeping you in place is a sense of "I've already given so much of my life to this, I can't just walk away now," that's an extremely common and well-documented pattern, not a personal failing. Psychologists call it the sunk cost fallacy: the more we've already invested in something, the harder it feels to leave, even once we're genuinely unsure it's right for us.

The years you've already spent are part of your story. They don't have to be the thing that decides your future. That distinction is worth sitting with for as long as you need to.

You can move slowly, and that's not the same as not moving at all

You don't have to go from "questioning" to "gone" in one step. A lot of people spend months, sometimes years, quietly gathering information, talking to people outside the group, or just letting themselves think honestly for the first time, long before anything visibly changes. That stage counts. It's not stalling. It's part of the process, even if nobody else can see it happening.

If you want somewhere low-pressure to start, reading through resources like this one, or looking into organisations like Faith to Faithless or Olive Leaf Network (both linked in our external resources below), doesn't commit you to anything. You can look, close the tab, and come back to it whenever you're ready.

If you decide you're not ready yet, that's a real answer too

Not everyone who questions ends up leaving, and staying, for now or for good, isn't a failure of this process either. This isn't a website trying to talk you into a decision. It's here for whichever direction you end up going, including "not yet," including "I'm still not sure," including changing your mind more than once.

Whenever, if ever, you do feel ready to think about the practical side of leaving, our guide on planning your exit without tipping anyone off covers that ground. But there's no rush to get there. This stage, the uncertain one, is allowed to take as long as it takes.

If this feels like too much to carry alone

If thinking about this is bringing up more than you feel able to carry alone, Faith to Faithless (Humanists UK) offers free, confidential support specifically for people in exactly this position - details in our external resources.
