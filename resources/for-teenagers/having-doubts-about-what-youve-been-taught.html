Having Doubts About What You've Been Taught? You're Not Alone

5 min read · Last updated August 2026

If you've started having questions, quiet ones, maybe ones you haven't said out loud to anyone yet, about things you were taught growing up, this is a completely normal part of becoming your own person. It doesn't mean something is wrong with you, and it doesn't mean you have to have any of it figured out right now.

This page is written directly for you, not for adults talking about you. So let's talk honestly.

Doubt is a normal part of growing up

Almost everyone, whatever they were taught growing up, goes through a stage of questioning it at some point, usually somewhere around your age. That's not a coincidence, it's part of how your brain naturally develops as a teenager, you start being able to think more independently, weigh things up for yourself, and notice when something doesn't quite add up. Having doubts doesn't mean you're being disloyal, ungrateful, or badly influenced. It usually just means your mind is doing exactly what it's supposed to do at this stage of your life.

You don't have to decide anything right now

This is probably the most important thing on this whole page. Questioning something doesn't require you to immediately reject it, and it doesn't require you to immediately announce anything to anyone. You're allowed to sit with uncertainty for a long time, months, years even, without it needing to resolve into a clear answer straight away. There's no deadline on figuring out what you believe.

It's okay if this feels scary

If you've been taught that doubting or questioning could have serious consequences, spiritually, or with your family, it makes complete sense that having these thoughts feels frightening rather than freeing. That fear is a real, understandable reaction, not a sign that your doubts are wrong. Try not to judge yourself for feeling scared. Most people in your exact position feel exactly the same way.

Talking to someone can help, even if it feels hard

Carrying this entirely alone is a lot for anyone, at any age. If there's an adult in your life you genuinely trust, a parent, a teacher, a school counsellor, another relative, it can really help to talk about how you're feeling, even in a small, careful way at first, without needing to share everything all at once.

If you don't feel able to talk to anyone in your immediate family or community about this yet, that's okay too. Childline (0800 1111, free, confidential, and they won't tell your parents unless you're in serious danger) is specifically there for young people to talk about absolutely anything that's on their mind, including things exactly like this. You don't have to have the right words ready, you can just start talking and see where it goes.

You're not the only one going through this

It can feel like you're the only person who's ever had these particular thoughts, especially if nobody around you seems to be questioning anything. You're genuinely not alone. A lot of people your age, and older, who grew up in similar environments, go through exactly this kind of questioning, even if it doesn't feel that way from where you're standing right now.

This website isn't going anywhere

If you want to come back and read more, whenever you're ready, this site will still be here. There's no pressure to do anything with what you're feeling right now, other than know that having questions is a normal, human thing, and you're allowed to have them.

There's no deadline on figuring out what you believe.

If this is feeling hard to cope with

Please talk to someone you trust, or contact Childline on 0800 1111 - it's free, confidential, and available any time. If calling feels too difficult right now, you can use Childline's online chat instead.
