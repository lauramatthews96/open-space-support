You Don't Have to Have It All Figured Out Yet

5 min read · Last updated August 2026

If you're questioning things you were taught growing up, there can be a real pressure, sometimes from other people, sometimes just from inside your own head, to reach a firm conclusion quickly. To decide, once and for all, what you believe, who you are, and what happens next. That pressure isn't fair, and it isn't necessary. You genuinely don't have to have this figured out yet.

Big questions take time, and that's normal

Questions about belief, identity, and what you actually think about the world are some of the biggest questions a person can ask. It would be strange if you worked through them in a week, or even a year. Adults spend their whole lives sometimes revisiting these same questions. Giving yourself permission to take your time isn't avoiding the question, it's actually the most honest way to approach something this important.

You can hold uncertainty without it being a problem

It might feel like you're supposed to land somewhere definite, either fully believing what you were taught or fully rejecting it, with nothing in between. Real life is usually messier than that, and it's genuinely okay to sit in a place of "I'm not sure yet" for a long time. Uncertainty isn't a failure to reach an answer. It's an honest, reasonable place to be while you're still thinking things through.

Try not to let fear make the decision for you, either way

Sometimes fear of consequences pushes people to publicly go along with something they privately doubt, just to avoid conflict. Sometimes fear of staying pushes people to reject everything quickly, before they've actually had time to think it through properly. Neither of these is really you making a free choice, they're both fear making the decision instead. Where you can, try to give yourself enough space and safety to think honestly, rather than being rushed by fear in either direction.

Your safety and wellbeing come first, always

While you're still living at home, still under the care of parents or guardians, your day to day safety and wellbeing matter more than resolving these big questions quickly. If exploring your doubts openly right now would put you at risk, of serious conflict, of being unsafe at home, it's okay to hold a lot of this privately for the time being, and to focus on getting through each day safely, while you continue thinking things through internally. This isn't giving up on your questions. It's taking care of yourself while you carry them.

Trusted adults exist for exactly this

If there's a school counsellor, a teacher, another relative, or any adult in your life who you feel could handle this conversation with care, even a small, cautious first conversation can make a real difference to how alone this feels. If nobody around you feels safe to talk to yet, Childline (0800 1111, free, confidential, available any time) is built specifically for conversations like this, and you don't need a fully formed question ready before you call or message them.

Whatever you eventually decide, it's allowed to be yours

Whether you end up believing something similar to what you were taught, something different, or something you're still working out even years from now, that's genuinely allowed to be your own honest conclusion, reached in your own time. Nobody else gets to rush that process for you, and you don't have to rush it for yourself either.

Uncertainty isn't a failure to reach an answer. It's an honest, reasonable place to be while you're still thinking things through.

If this feels like too much to carry alone

Please reach out to a trusted adult in your life, or contact Childline on 0800 1111 - it's free, confidential, and available any time. If calling feels too difficult right now, you can use Childline's online chat instead.
