Finding LGBTQ+ Community When You Missed Out on It Growing Up

6 min read · Last updated August 2026

A lot of LGBTQ+ people build community gradually, through school, friendship groups, culture, and years of small, low-stakes exposure to LGBTQ+ spaces and people. If you grew up in a high-control religious environment, you may be starting this later, as an adult, without any of that gradual build-up, and possibly without much reference point at all for what LGBTQ+ community and culture even look like. That's a real and specific starting point, and it's worth having a practical way in, rather than being handed vague advice to "find your people" with no clear sense of how.

Why this can feel more daunting than it should

If you missed the years most people use to gradually pick up references, language, and a sense of belonging within LGBTQ+ culture, it's genuinely common to feel like an outsider even within spaces that are meant to be welcoming to you specifically. You might not know the terminology, the history, the shared cultural touchpoints that others seem to already know. This can create a strange, doubled sense of not fully belonging, not in your family or former community, and initially, not quite in LGBTQ+ spaces either, simply from unfamiliarity rather than any actual barrier to belonging there.

It's worth naming this directly: not knowing the references yet doesn't mean you don't belong. It means you're arriving at a point most people arrive at earlier, and there's no penalty for that, even if it feels awkward at first.

Starting points that don't require much prior knowledge

LGBTQ+ helplines and organisations, like Switchboard LGBT+ (see our external resources), aren't just for crises, many offer general information, local group listings, and a genuinely low-pressure way to ask basic questions you might feel embarrassed asking anyone else.

LGBTQ+ specific meetup and social groups. Many cities and towns have regular, casual social meetups specifically for LGBTQ+ people, often themed around an activity (a walking group, a book club, a games night) rather than requiring you to already know how to "network" in queer spaces. Starting with an activity-based group can feel more manageable than a purely social event, since there's a built-in focus beyond just socialising.

Online communities, as a lower-pressure first step. Before attending anything in person, LGBTQ+ forums and online communities can offer a way to observe, ask questions, and gradually get familiar with language and culture, entirely at your own pace and anonymously if you'd prefer, before taking anything further into in-person spaces.

LGBTQ+ affirming faith or spirituality groups, if that's relevant to you. If you haven't left faith or spirituality altogether, just the specific group or its teachings on identity, there are LGBTQ+ affirming religious and spiritual communities that may offer a way to hold both parts of your identity together, rather than feeling like you have to choose. We cover faith and identity further in our LGBTQ+ resources if that's relevant to you.

Learning the culture without feeling behind

It's genuinely fine to ask questions, even ones that might feel obvious to others. Most LGBTQ+ people understand, directly or indirectly, that not everyone had the same access to community and culture growing up, and a good community will generally be patient with genuine curiosity rather than treating it as a failure to already know something.

You also don't need to catch up on everything at once. Language, culture, and history within LGBTQ+ communities are broad and constantly evolving, even people who've been part of these communities for years are still learning. Give yourself permission to pick things up gradually, through actual exposure and conversation, rather than trying to study up on it all in advance before you feel ready to show up anywhere.

Pace yourself, this doesn't need to happen all at once

Building genuine community takes time regardless of your starting point, and starting later than most doesn't mean the process moves any faster once you begin. It's completely reasonable to try one small step, one online community, one local meetup, one conversation, and let it develop from there, rather than expecting yourself to arrive at deep, established friendships immediately.

You're not behind, you're just on a different timeline

It's easy to compare yourself to people who've had years longer to build community and feel like you're starting from a deficit. But community built later in life, deliberately and with real effort, tends to be genuinely meaningful, precisely because you're choosing it consciously, in a way many people never have to. There's no correct age or timeline for finding your people. There's just the actual process of finding them, whenever you're ready to begin, and plenty of people have started exactly where you are now and built real, lasting community from it.

Not knowing the references yet doesn't mean you don't belong. It means you're arriving at a point most people arrive at earlier.

Finding community and support

Switchboard LGBT+ (0800 0119 100, free, confidential) offers information and support, including help finding local LGBTQ+ groups and services, and our external resources below include further options for building community.
