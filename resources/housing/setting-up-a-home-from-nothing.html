Setting Up a Home From Nothing

5 min read · Last updated August 2026

Moving into a first place of your own is a genuinely big milestone, and it can also be daunting when you're starting with genuinely little, no furniture handed down, no savings built up for it, no prior experience of what you actually need versus what you can live without for now. This is more manageable than it feels at the start, and most people build a home gradually, not all at once.

Start with what you actually need, not what a home is "supposed" to have

It's easy to feel like a home needs to look a certain way immediately, fully furnished, matching, complete. In reality, most people build their space up over months or years, and there's no shame in a first home being sparse while you gradually add to it. Focus on genuine needs first: somewhere to sleep, basic kitchen essentials, and a way to keep warm, comfort and decoration can come later, as money allows.

The genuinely essential basics

A bed, even a simple one. Sleep quality affects everything else, so this is worth prioritising over almost anything.

Basic kitchen items, a kettle, a pan or two, a few plates and cutlery, something to store food in. You don't need a full set from day one, just enough to prepare simple meals.

Bedding and towels. Easy to forget until you actually need them.

Basic cleaning supplies. A few essentials go a long way, and keeping a new space clean from the start makes it feel more like home faster.

A way to lock and secure your space properly, if this isn't already sorted as part of your tenancy.

Where to find things cheaply, or free

Freecycle and Freegle connect people giving away items they no longer need, genuinely useful for furniture, kitchenware, and other essentials, entirely free.

Facebook Marketplace and local selling groups often have furniture and household items at low prices, particularly from people moving house themselves and wanting a quick sale rather than the hassle of transporting things.

Charity shops, particularly larger ones or dedicated furniture charity shops (British Heart Foundation runs furniture and electrical stores specifically), offer genuinely low prices on furniture and household basics, often with delivery available for a small fee.

Local council or charity furniture schemes. Many areas have schemes specifically supporting people setting up a home from very little, sometimes linked to housing services or local charities, worth asking your council's housing team about directly.

Ask, even if it feels awkward. If you know anyone with items they're not using, spare kitchenware, an old sofa, it's genuinely fine to ask. Most people are glad to see things go to someone who'll actually use them rather than sitting in storage.

Prioritise, rather than trying to do everything at once

Week one priorities: somewhere to sleep, basic hygiene and kitchen essentials, secure locks, and knowing where your nearest shops and services are.

Month one priorities: enough furniture and storage to function comfortably, sorting bills and any necessary paperwork, settling into a basic routine.

Beyond that: everything else, decoration, comfort items, anything beyond function, can be added gradually as your situation allows, with no pressure to rush it.

It's okay for this to take time

There's no deadline by which a home needs to feel fully complete. A sparse space that's genuinely yours, safe and secure, is a real achievement, even before it's fully furnished or decorated the way you might eventually want. Building it up slowly, piece by piece, as you're able to, is a completely normal and reasonable way to do this, not a sign you're behind or doing it wrong.

This is genuinely yours

However modest it starts out, a space that's entirely your own, with nobody else's rules governing it, is worth recognising as a real achievement in itself. The furniture and finishing touches will come, gradually, as they're able to. What matters most at the start is simply that it's safe, and it's yours.

If your housing situation is urgent

If you don't yet have somewhere safe to stay, or you're at risk of losing the place you're in, start with what to do if you're at risk of homelessness. Shelter's free emergency helpline is 0808 800 4444 (England and Scotland, Monday to Friday). In Wales, Shelter Cymru's advice line is 08000 495 495; in Northern Ireland, Housing Rights is on 028 9024 5640. If you're in immediate danger, call 999.
