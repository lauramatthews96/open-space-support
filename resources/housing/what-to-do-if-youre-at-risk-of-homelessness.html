What to Do If You're at Risk of Homelessness

6 min read · Last updated August 2026

If you're worried about losing your current place to stay, whether that's because you've left, been asked to leave, or your current situation has simply become unsustainable, it's worth knowing clearly that there is real, practical help available, and that reaching out for it early makes a genuine difference to your options.

This isn't a situation to wait out privately until it becomes a crisis. The earlier you get in touch with the right services, the more choices you generally have.

You have a legal right to help, in most cases

In England, Scotland, and Wales, local councils have a legal duty to help people who are homeless or at risk of becoming homeless within 56 days, regardless of whether you're considered a "priority" case for actual accommodation. This means you can, and should, contact your local council's housing options team even before you've actually lost your accommodation, if you know it's coming. Getting in touch early genuinely widens what they're able to do for you.

You don't need to have exhausted every other option first, and you don't need to justify or prove your situation extensively before they'll help. Contacting them is the right first step, not a last resort.

What to actually do, in order

Contact your local council's housing options team as soon as you know you're at risk. You can find your local council's contact details through gov.uk, and most have a dedicated homelessness or housing options line. Explain your situation honestly and clearly, when you expect to lose your current accommodation, and why.

If it's outside office hours or an emergency, use Shelter's emergency helpline (0808 800 4444, free, England), which can advise you on your rights and immediate options, including how to access emergency accommodation if you have genuinely nowhere safe to stay tonight.

If you're already sleeping rough, or about to be, StreetLink can connect you with local outreach services who can help directly, often within a day or two of being contacted.

Ask about interim accommodation. If your situation qualifies, councils can often provide interim, temporary accommodation while your full homelessness application is assessed, so you're not left with nowhere to stay during that process.

What information helps when you contact them

Having a few things ready, even roughly, makes the process faster, though don't let missing any of this stop you from calling.

Your current living situation and why it's ending. Any relevant dates, when you need to leave by, if known. Any income or benefits you currently receive. Whether you have children or any health conditions that might affect what support you're eligible for. Any safety concerns relevant to your situation, this affects what kind of accommodation might be appropriate for you.

If you're facing this because of shunning or family estrangement specifically

If your housing situation has become unstable because of shunning, being asked to leave a family home due to leaving a religious group, or similar circumstances, it's worth mentioning this clearly when you contact housing services, since it's relevant context for understanding your situation, even though it doesn't need to be explained in more detail than you're comfortable with. The Family Survival Trust's resources (see our external resources) specifically address housing options for exactly this situation.

You're not asking for too much

It's common to feel a strong reluctance to ask for this kind of help, a sense that you should be able to manage this yourself, or that you're taking a resource meant for someone in a worse situation. Housing support exists specifically for people in your position, and using it doesn't take anything away from anyone else who might also need it. Reaching out early, before things become a full crisis, is exactly what these services are designed for.

This is a solvable situation, even when it doesn't feel like it

Housing instability is genuinely frightening, and it's completely understandable if it's occupying most of your thoughts right now. But it is a solvable situation, with real services built specifically to help, and reaching out is the single most useful thing you can do, more useful than trying to work it all out alone before asking anyone for support.

Reaching out early, before things become a full crisis, is exactly what these services are designed for.

If you have nowhere safe to stay tonight

Please contact Shelter's emergency helpline (0808 800 4444, free) or your local council's emergency housing line as a priority. In Wales, Shelter Cymru's advice line is 08000 495 495; in Northern Ireland, Housing Rights is on 028 9024 5640. If you're in immediate danger, contact emergency services on 999.
